About

I am a doctoral student, working toward a Doctor of Science, in Professor Toru Morishita's theoretical atomic physics group at the Institute for Advanced Science, The University of Electro-Communications (UEC), Tokyo, which I joined in 2022. I work at the meeting point of numerical methods, formal quantum mechanics and atomic physics. Most of my work is on two-electron systems such as helium, where electron correlation produces doubly excited and autoionizing states that simple one-electron pictures cannot explain.

I develop and validate my own codes, mostly in Fortran with Python for reference calculations and analysis, and I compare full numerical solutions of the time-dependent Schrödinger equation with analytic and semi-classical theories. On the nuclear side, I study α decay as quantum tunnelling and how an intense laser field changes it.

I also work with collaborators on physics research and academic writing, and I have a long-standing interest in teacher training for English as a foreign language.

How I work

Every result climbs a validation ladder before I trust it. Independent methods have to agree at each rung, and convergence tests decide whether a number is physics or an artefact of the box.

  1. 1
    Analytic limitsExactly solvable cases and known values
  2. 2
    Independent referenceNumPy/SciPy and sparse Arnoldi solvers
  3. 3
    Production codeModular Fortran 90 with built-in self-tests
  4. 4
    ConvergenceGrid, box size and complex-scaling angle scans
  5. 5
    InterpretationTesting models against the numbers to rule mechanisms in or out

Interactive physics

Two ideas from my research that you can play with.

Fano resonance

A doubly excited state interferes with the ionization continuum, giving an asymmetric line. Its shape is set by the parameter q.

σ(ε) ∝ (q + ε)² / (1 + ε²). Zero at ε = −q, peak at ε = 1/q. The dashed line is the Lorentzian limit.

α decay as tunnelling

An α particle trapped in the nucleus leaks through the Coulomb barrier. A laser field adds a term F r cos Ωt that shakes the barrier.

Schematic, not to scale. R: nuclear radius, rt: outer turning point, Q: decay energy.
